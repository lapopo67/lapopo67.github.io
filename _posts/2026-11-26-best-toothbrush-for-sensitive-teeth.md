If brushing makes your teeth twinge or your gums feel sore, the right electric toothbrush can make the two-minute routine a lot more comfortable. The features that matter most for sensitive mouths are simple: a gentle or "sensitive" mode, adjustable intensity, soft brush heads, and a pressure sensor that warns you when you're pushing too hard.

For this guide we compared the latest electric toothbrush roundups from Tom's Guide, NBC Select's oral-care product list and Forbes Vetted, and pulled out the models reviewers describe as gentle on teeth and gums. We didn't test these brushes ourselves; findings are attributed to the publications that did.

One important note before you shop: a new brush can make brushing more comfortable, but it doesn't treat the cause of sensitivity. Sensitivity that lasts, or gums that bleed regularly, are worth raising with your dentist.

## Why gentle brushing matters

The ADA recommends a soft-bristled brush, brushing twice a day for two minutes with fluoride toothpaste, cleaning between your teeth daily, and replacing your brush head about every three months or sooner if the bristles fray. Scrubbing hard doesn't clean better. Reviewers at NBC Select point out that medium or hard bristles can be rough on teeth and gums, and that pressure sensors help guard against that.

An electric brush also does the scrubbing for you. With either a sonic (vibrating) or oscillating (rotating) brush, the job is to guide the head slowly along the gumline and let the motor do the work.

## How to choose

**A real sensitive mode or low intensity.** One of the most useful features is a way to dial the power down. Look for a named "Sensitive" mode or several intensity levels. Tom's Guide notes that some high-frequency brushes can feel harsh on sensitive gums, so a lower setting helps.

**Pressure sensor.** Pressing too hard is a common reason brushing feels uncomfortable. A sensor that lights up, buzzes or, like the Suri 2.0, reduces power when you push gives you instant feedback while you build a lighter habit.

**Soft brush heads.** Check that soft or extra-soft heads are available for your brush, and budget for replacements about every three months.

**Timer and pacing.** A two-minute timer with 30-second buzzes helps you brush evenly instead of scrubbing one sore spot for too long.

**Sonic or oscillating.** Both styles clean well when used properly. Some people find sonic brushes feel gentler; others prefer an oscillating head. If you can, try your brush on its lowest setting for the first week or two while your mouth adjusts.

**Battery and charging.** Battery life ranges from about two weeks to over a month. Longer is handy if you travel.

## Tips for brushing with sensitive teeth

- Hold the bristles at a slight angle toward the gumline and glide slowly; don't scrub back and forth.
- Start on the lowest intensity and only increase it if it feels comfortable.
- Use a fluoride toothpaste. Toothpastes for sensitive teeth are widely available; ask your dentist whether one is right for you.
- Keep cleaning between your teeth daily with floss, interdental brushes or a water flosser.
