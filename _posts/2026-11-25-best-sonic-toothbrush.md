Electric toothbrushes come in two main styles. Oscillating brushes (mostly Oral-B) have a small round head that rotates back and forth. Sonic brushes (Philips Sonicare, Waterpik, AquaSonic, Suri and others) have a conventional brush-shaped head that vibrates side to side very quickly. Many people find sonic brushes feel gentler and quieter, and the familiar head shape makes the switch from a manual brush easy.

For this guide we compared the current electric toothbrush roundups from TechRadar, Expert Reviews, NBC Select and CNN Underscored and pulled out the sonic models they recommend. We didn't test these brushes ourselves; findings are attributed to the publications that did.

## Sonic vs. oscillating: does it matter?

A Cochrane systematic review of powered toothbrushes found that, overall, electric brushes reduce plaque and gum inflammation more than manual brushing. A separate Cochrane review comparing types of powered brushes found some evidence that rotation-oscillation heads removed slightly more plaque and gingivitis than side-to-side brushes in short trials, but the difference was small, its clinical importance unclear, and the trials were limited. That review is also more than a decade old.

The practical takeaway: either style works well when you use it properly. The ADA's basics matter more than the motor: brush twice a day for two minutes with fluoride toothpaste, use soft bristles, clean between your teeth daily, and replace the brush head about every three months or sooner if the bristles fray. Pick the brush that feels comfortable enough that you'll use it every day.

## How to choose

**Pressure sensor.** Brushing too hard is a common habit, and it can wear on gums and enamel over time. A sensor that lights up or buzzes when you press too hard is one of the most useful features, especially if you're switching from a manual brush.

**Timer and quadrant pacer.** Nearly all of these brushes run for two minutes and pause or buzz every 30 seconds. That simple feature does a lot to help you brush long enough and evenly.

**Modes and intensity.** Most people need one good mode. A lower intensity or "easy start" setting helps while you get used to the vibration. Extra modes like whitening or gum care are nice to have but not essential.

**Battery and charging.** Battery life ranges from about two weeks to well over a month. If you travel, look for USB-C charging or a case that doubles as a charger.

**Replacement head cost.** Brush heads are the ongoing cost. Check the price of a multi-pack before you buy, and plan to replace heads about every three months.

**ADA Seal.** Some brushes carry the ADA Seal of Acceptance, which means the ADA has evaluated the maker's data on safety and effectiveness. It's a helpful signal, though brushes without it can still work well.

## Tips for using a sonic brush

- Hold the bristles at a slight angle toward the gumline and let the brush do the work; don't scrub back and forth like a manual brush.
- Glide slowly from tooth to tooth, spending about 30 seconds on each quarter of your mouth.
- Keep cleaning between your teeth every day with floss, interdental brushes or a water flosser; a toothbrush can't reach those spaces.
