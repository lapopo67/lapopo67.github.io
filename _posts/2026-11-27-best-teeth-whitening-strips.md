Whitening strips are the easiest way to brighten your smile at home: you press a thin, gel-coated strip onto your teeth, wait, peel it off and repeat for a week or two. They cost far less than a dentist's treatment, but they vary a lot in strength, grip and how kind they are to sensitive teeth.

For this guide we compared the latest whitening strip roundups from NBC Select (updated November 2026) and Fortune (October 2026), and checked the safety basics against guidance from the American Dental Association (ADA) and Cleveland Clinic. We didn't test these products ourselves; findings are attributed to the publications that did.

A quick reality check first: strips only lighten natural teeth. Crowns, veneers and fillings won't change color, and teeth that look brown or gray may have a cause whitening can't fix. That's why both the ADA and Cleveland Clinic suggest talking to your dentist before you start.

## How whitening strips work

Most strips use hydrogen peroxide or carbamide peroxide, which breaks down into hydrogen peroxide. The ADA notes that over-the-counter products use lower concentrations than the ones dentists supply, so they generally take longer to reach the same result. Cleveland Clinic says strips typically lighten teeth within about two weeks.

The most common side effect is temporary tooth sensitivity, and gum irritation can happen too, especially if gel spreads onto the gums. According to the ADA, sensitivity with strips or trays often shows up a few days in and usually settles quickly. Peroxide-free strips are gentler but, as NBC Select points out, usually less effective.

## How to choose

**Look for the ADA Seal.** Cleveland Clinic recommends choosing products with the ADA Seal of Acceptance, which means the product has been reviewed for safety and effectiveness. NBC notes Crest 3D Whitestrips Professional Effects carries it.

**Peroxide or peroxide-free.** Peroxide strips generally work better and faster. Peroxide-free strips are gentler but usually give more subtle results. NBC's dentist sources note that lower concentrations are kinder to teeth but work more slowly.

**Grip and fit.** Strips that slip let gel reach your gums and miss parts of your teeth. Reviewers repeatedly mention grip as the difference between a good and a frustrating product.

**Wear time and course length.** Sessions range from about 15 to 45 minutes, and a full course can last from one to three weeks. Pick a routine you'll actually finish.

**Coverage.** Strips mainly cover the front teeth you see when you smile. Trays cover more teeth but use a different format.

**When a dentist is the better option.** If you want a big change, have deep or uneven staining, or have crowns or veneers on front teeth, a dentist can check what's causing the discoloration and offer custom trays or in-office whitening. The ADA notes that dentist-supplied products use higher concentrations and can work faster, and custom trays fit better, which helps keep gel off your gums.

## Tips for using whitening strips

- Read and follow the instructions exactly. Cleveland Clinic stresses not overdoing it, since wear time and frequency vary by product.
- If sensitivity develops, take a break or switch to a milder product, and mention it to your dentist.
- Avoid DIY whitening such as lemon juice, vinegar, charcoal or household hydrogen peroxide. The ADA and Cleveland Clinic warn these lack evidence or can harm enamel and gums.
- Whitening isn't recommended for children; the ADA notes that pediatric dentists discourage full-mouth bleaching for kids.
- Keep up your regular routine: brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste, clean between your teeth daily and keep up regular dental checkups.

For dramatic results or tricky stains, a dentist's whitening is still the better choice.
