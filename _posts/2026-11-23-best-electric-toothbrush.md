Electric toothbrushes remove plaque more consistently than most people manage by hand, mainly because they do the motion for you and time your two minutes. But the jump from a $40 brush to a $300 one buys mostly app features, not dramatically cleaner teeth.

This guide combines the 2026 test results published by TechRadar, T3, Live Science, NBC Select and Consumer Reports with the basics of good brushing technique.

## What actually matters

Before looking at models, these are the features that change results:

1. **Soft bristles.** Medium and hard bristles don't clean better; they can wear enamel and irritate gums. Choose soft heads.
2. **A two-minute timer** with a 30-second "quadrant" pacer. Almost every electric brush has this, and it's the single biggest upgrade over manual brushing for most people.
3. **A pressure sensor.** Pressing too hard is one of the most common causes of gum recession. A sensor that warns you is worth paying for.
4. **Affordable replacement heads.** Heads should be replaced about every three months, so their price matters more over time than the handle's price.

Smart features (apps, AI position tracking, many modes) are nice to have but optional.

**Sonic vs. oscillating:** Philips Sonicare-style brushes vibrate side to side at high speed; Oral-B-style brushes have a small round head that rotates back and forth. Both clean well when used correctly. Pick the feel you prefer.

## Brushing tips that matter more than the brush

- Brush twice a day for two full minutes; let the brush do the work and **don't scrub**.
- Angle the bristles toward the gumline and move slowly from tooth to tooth.
- Replace the head about every three months, or sooner if the bristles splay.
- Clean between your teeth daily (floss, interdental brushes or a water flosser); no toothbrush reaches there.
- Look for the ADA Seal of Acceptance if you want extra reassurance, though brushes without it can still be effective.

*This guide is general information, not personal dental advice. If you have sensitive teeth, gum disease, implants or orthodontic work, ask your dentist which brush and head type suit you.*
